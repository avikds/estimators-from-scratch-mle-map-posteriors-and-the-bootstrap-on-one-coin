Estimators from Scratch: MLE, MAP, Posteriors and the Bootstrap on One Coin

How it was built

Part 1: The Coin, Its Likelihood and the MLE

Turn the table log into sufficient statistics, write the Bernoulli negative log likelihood, find its minimum both in closed form and with a golden-section search, and extend maximum likelihood to a three-outcome categorical (heads, tails, edge) and to a Gaussian model of the coin's weight (Murphy 4.2.3-4.2.5).

Part 2: The Zero-Count Problem and MAP Estimation

Add a Beta prior to the likelihood to get the MAP estimate and add-one smoothing, score predictions by their log loss on future flips, and measure by simulation how often the MLE calls a future flip impossible (Murphy 4.5 and 4.5.1, the black swan paradox).

Part 3: The Beta-Bernoulli Posterior

Build the conjugate posterior of the coin: the Beta density, posterior mean, mode and variance, the marginal likelihood, sequential one-step-ahead predictions, the beta-binomial predictive against the plug-in, the regularized incomplete beta function, and a mixture-of-Betas prior for a casino that may be cheating (Murphy 4.6.2).

Part 4: Credible Intervals

Invert your Beta cdf into quantiles, compute central and highest-density 95% credible intervals for a Beta posterior, and find the highest-density interval from a bag of samples (Murphy 4.6.6, Figure 4.18).

Part 5: Approximate Inference on 10 Heads and 1 Tail

Pretend the conjugate posterior is unknown and approximate it with a grid, a Laplace approximation in theta, a Laplace approximation in logit space and a random-walk Metropolis sampler, then measure each one's total variation distance to the exact posterior and its estimate of the evidence (Murphy 4.6.8, Figure 4.22).

Part 6: The Frequentist View: Bootstrap, Coverage and Bias-Variance

Treat the data as random and the coin as fixed: bootstrap the MLE and compare it with posterior samples, compute the exact sampling distribution of any estimator, the exact coverage of Wald and Jeffreys intervals, and the bias, variance and mean squared error of shrinkage estimators (Murphy 4.7).
